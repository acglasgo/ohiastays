Top 5 Hikes
in Austin

Creekside trails and greenbelts, with the dog rules spelled out.

The best hikes near downtown Austin for a South Austin stay are the Mary Moore Searight off-leash trail, Onion Creek Greenbelt, Turkey Creek Trail at Emma Long, Shoal Creek Trail at Pease Park, and the Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail from Zilker. Four have off-leash dog areas, and drive times run 5 to 35 minutes from the house.

Austin's trails run through greenbelts and creeks inside the city, so a good walk is rarely far. We favor dog-friendly trails and say where dogs must stay leashed. Closures and detours are current as of the date below, so check the city page before you drive. We take no commissions. Drive times are from the house.

No. 01

Mary Moore Searight Metro Park Off-Leash Trail

Free 10 min drive off-leash dog romp, easy loop

A 1.22-mile dirt trail on the north end of a 300-acre south Austin park, set aside by the city for dogs to run off leash through live oak and cedar. It is the best pick here for a dog that needs real exercise. The area is not fenced, so keep your dog in sight and sound range, and use the S. 1st Street entrance, where parking is easiest. Leashes are required in the rest of the park.

City parks are open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. unless posted.

Off-leash on the designated trail only; leash your dog to and from it and everywhere else in the park. Dogs must stay in sight and sound control; dogs in heat are not allowed.

 Pro Tip

Locals say the trail turns to sticky mud after rain, so give it a day or two to dry and bring a towel for the car.

No. 02

Onion Creek Greenbelt

Free 5 min drive flat creekside walk with dog

A 250-acre creekside greenbelt in south Austin with a 2.16-mile hike-and-bike trail, shaded flats, a creek crossing and one of the city's off-leash areas. Mornings are quiet and the trail is flat enough for older dogs and small kids. Parking is in a lot off Onion Creek Drive that also serves the off-leash area. Expect mud and a swollen creek after heavy rain.

City parks are open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. unless posted.

The greenbelt has an off-leash area near the parking lot; keep dogs on a 6-foot leash on trails and elsewhere in the park, and leashed to and from the off-leash area.

 Pro Tip

Check the creek before you go; crossings can be impassable for a day or two after storms.

No. 03

Turkey Creek Trail (Emma Long Metro Park)

$ 35 min drive shady off-leash creek hike

A shaded 1.92-mile out-and-back dirt trail along Turkey Creek under cedar and oak, listed by the city as a hike and off-leash trail. It is rocky in spots, not stroller friendly, and busy with dogs on weekends. The trail sits inside Emma Long Metropolitan Park, which charges a per-car fee ($5 Monday to Thursday, $10 Friday to Sunday and holidays), and weekend day passes are sold in advance.

Park open 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. year-round (city page).

The city lists Turkey Creek Trail as hike and off-leash. The rest of Emma Long Park, including picnic sites and the beach, requires leashed pets.

 Pro Tip

On Friday to Sunday and holidays, buy the day pass online ahead of time, since the park caps entry at 250 cars.

No. 04

Shoal Creek Trail at Pease Park

Free 15 min drive easy downtown walk with dog

The city's Shoal Creek Greenbelt Trail runs about 6.8 miles through central Austin, and the stretch through Pease Park is the easiest way in from downtown, with a paved path beside the creek and shade trees. Start at the Kingsbury Street entrance and walk north, passing play areas and open lawn. The only off-leash stretch is between 24th and 29th Streets; closures further north may force a sidewalk detour.

City parks are open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. unless posted.

Dogs must be leashed except in the off-leash area between 24th and 29th Streets (Pease Park Conservancy, Austin Parks Foundation).

 Pro Tip

Check shoalcreekconservancy.org/trail-closures before heading north of 29th Street; part of the trail is under repair.

No. 05

Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail (Zilker start)

Free 15 min drive skyline lakeside walk, strollers

The city's Butler Hike and Bike Trail loops Lady Bird Lake for about 10 miles on crushed granite and boardwalk, with skyline views. From Zilker Metropolitan Park, walk east toward the boardwalk or west toward Barton Springs. Leashed dogs are welcome, shade is limited on summer afternoons, and the surface is stroller friendly. Weekend parking at Zilker costs $3 an hour in season.

City parks are open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. unless posted.

Dogs must be on a leash of 6 feet or shorter; a few small leash-free dog spots exist. The city warns that harmful algae in Lady Bird Lake can harm or kill dogs (summer and fall): keep dogs out of the water.

 Pro Tip

Park free at a quieter lot on a weekday, or bike or rideshare on weekends and festival days.

Questions about Austin hikes

Which Austin trails allow dogs off-leash?

Mary Moore Searight's off-leash trail, Turkey Creek Trail at Emma Long, the Shoal Creek stretch between 24th and 29th Streets, and an off-leash area near the Onion Creek parking lot. Dogs must be leashed everywhere else in each park.

Are any Austin trails closed right now?

A section of Shoal Creek Trail from Shoal Creek Blvd to Janet Fish Bridge is closed for repairs with no confirmed reopening date. The Butler Trail has a north-shore detour through March 2027, and Emma Long's water is off until about mid-January 2027.

Do Austin trails cost anything?

Most city trails are free. Emma Long Metropolitan Park, where Turkey Creek Trail is, charges $5 a car Monday to Thursday and $10 Friday to Sunday and holidays.

Can my dog swim in Lady Bird Lake?

The city warns that harmful algae in Lady Bird Lake can harm or kill dogs, mostly in summer and fall. Keep dogs out of the water along the Butler Trail.
