Top 5 Swimming &
Paddling Spots

Springs, pools and lakes. Honest about winter.

The best places to swim and paddle near downtown Austin are Barton Springs Pool, Deep Eddy Pool, Rowing Dock paddle rentals on Lady Bird Lake, Emma Long Metropolitan Park on Lake Austin, and Bob Wentz Park at Windy Point on Lake Travis. The water is cold in winter and several spots have closures this season, so check each card first.

Austin has no beach, but it has springs, pools and lakes. In winter the water is cold and a few spots are closed for repairs, so this list is honest about what is open. Dates and hours come from each operator's own page as of the date below. We take no commissions. Drive times are from the house.

No. 01

Barton Springs Pool

Free 15 min drive Spring-fed swim, all year

A three-acre, spring-fed pool in Zilker Park, with a grassy hillside, old pecan and cypress trees and a rocky bottom you can feel underfoot. The water holds around 68 to 70 degrees all year, so it feels bracing in winter and a relief in August. Bring a towel, goggles and a picnic blanket for the lawn above the pool. Parking at the north entrance fills up early on sunny weekends, so arrive in the morning or walk in from the trail.

Open daily 5 a.m. to 10 p.m., but closed Thursdays 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. for cleaning; closed Thanksgiving and Christmas Day, early close Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve. Water is about 68 to 70 degrees all year, so expect cold winter swims. The City lists admission as free until March 13, 2027, then adults pay $5 (Austin residents) or $9 (non-residents).

No dogs allowed in the pool area (the City lists pets as not permitted). Dogs are welcome on leash on the Zilker Park lawns and the Barton Creek trails nearby.

 Pro Tip

Thursday is cleaning day, so go any other day or come for the 7 to 10 pm guarded swim.

No. 02

Rowing Dock paddle rentals on Lady Bird Lake

$$ 15 min drive Flat-water paddling, skyline views

A small, local rental dock at the west edge of Zilker Park, on the quiet upstream end of Lady Bird Lake. You can rent kayaks, canoes, pedal kayaks and stand up paddleboards, then drift toward the Pfluger Bridge and the downtown skyline or head the other way under MoPac. The water is flat with no motorboats, which suits first-time paddlers. A small free lot fills quickly, and public parking is a 5 to 10 minute walk east.

Open daily 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., last boat out at 5 p.m., per the dock's site. No separate winter hours are posted, and it closes in bad weather (it posted a weather closure on Nov 24), so check the site or call before you drive over. Rates run about $20 to $30 an hour.

 Pro Tip

Rentals are 50% off after 4 pm, and the last boat goes out at 5.

No. 03

Deep Eddy Pool

Free 15 min drive Easy family dip, old-school pool

Texas's oldest swimming pool, built in 1915 beside the river and filled each day with untreated aquifer water. It is a shallow-ended, tree-shaded concrete pool with a big lawn, a bathhouse and a small splash area. It is a short stroll to the hike and bike trail and several cafes in Old West Austin. It is smaller and calmer than Barton Springs, and a good pick for kids and a quick dip.

Winter hours (to Mar 13, 2027): 9 a.m. to 7 p.m., closed Tuesdays for cleaning. Closed for valve repairs Nov 30 to Dec 21, 2026, and closed Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and New Year's Day (4 p.m. close on New Year's Eve). Water is unheated, so it is cold in winter. The City lists admission as free for now.

 Pro Tip

The pool opens only once it has filled with water, so allow for a late start and arrive after 10 a.m.

No. 04

Bob Wentz Park at Windy Point

$ 40 min drive Lake Travis swim and paddle

A Travis County park on Lake Travis with a rocky shoreline, steps and ledges for swimming, picnic shelters and a ramp for non-motorized boats. It is known for wind, so you will often see windsurfers and kite boarders on the water and cliff views from The Point. Bring cash for the entry booth, water shoes for the rocks, and a lunch. It is clothing-required, unlike its neighbor Hippie Hollow.

Open daily 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., last entry 30 minutes before close. $5 a person (seniors $3, children 12 and under free), cash only. Lake Travis water is cold in winter, so the swim is a brave one. The county notes the dock at the non-motorized ramp is closed in high water, and the lake level changes.

Leashed dogs only. Off-leash pets are prohibited, and pets are not allowed on The Point.

 Pro Tip

Cash only at the gate, and the park can hit capacity on holidays and warm weekends, so go early.

No. 05

Emma Long Metropolitan Park

$ 35 min drive Beach-style swim on Lake Austin

A city park on Lake Austin west of downtown, with a beach-entry swimming area, grassy picnic spots, a nature trail and boat ramps. The lake is calm and green, with limestone bluffs along the shore. It is the nearest thing Austin has to a real beach, with sand under your feet. Pack a lunch and water shoes. The park fills at 250 cars, so arrive before noon on busy days.

Open daily 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. $5 a car Monday to Thursday, $10 Friday to Sunday and holidays (advance online pass). Water utilities are shut off until about Jan 15, 2027, with portable toilets on site and no showers. The lake is cold in winter.

Dogs welcome on leash and under your control at all times.

 Pro Tip

Friday to Sunday and holiday passes are sold online up to 14 days ahead and can sell out.

Questions about swimming and paddling in Austin

Can you swim in Austin in winter?

Barton Springs stays about 68 to 70 degrees all year, so a winter swim is cold but possible. Deep Eddy's water is unheated, the lakes are cold, and Emma Long has no showers until about mid-January 2027.

Which Austin pools are closed right now?

Deep Eddy Pool is closed Nov 30 to Dec 21, 2026 for valve repairs, and on Tuesdays for cleaning. Barton Springs closes Thursdays from 9 am to 7 pm for cleaning, and on Thanksgiving and Christmas Day.

Do Austin swimming spots allow dogs?

No dogs are allowed in the Barton Springs pool area. Leashed dogs are welcome at Bob Wentz Park, but not on The Point, and at Emma Long Metropolitan Park.

How much do these swimming spots cost?

Barton Springs admission is free until March 13, 2027, then $5 for Austin residents or $9 for non-residents. Bob Wentz Park is $5 a person in cash, and Emma Long is $5 a car Monday to Thursday and $10 Friday to Sunday. Rowing Dock rentals run about $20 to $30 an hour.
