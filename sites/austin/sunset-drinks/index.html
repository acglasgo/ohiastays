Top 5 Sunset Spots
& Live Music

Family-friendly golden hour: a brewery farm, two free viewpoints and two classic evenings.

The best sunset and live-music spots near downtown Austin are Jester King Brewery for a family farm sunset, Mount Bonnell for a free view, Auditorium Shores for a skyline picnic, The Broken Spoke for dinner and a two-step lesson, and Cidercade for an arcade night. In winter the sun sets around 5:30 pm, so arrive mid-afternoon.

These are family-friendly sunset stops rather than late-night bars: a brewery on a farm, two free viewpoints and two classic Austin evenings. Austin's well-known bat watch at the South Congress Bridge runs roughly March to November, so it is off this winter. Age and dog notes are on each card. We take no commissions. No. 01

Jester King Brewery

$$ 30 min drive Family sunset on a farm

A working farm brewery on 41 Hill Country acres west of town, with more than 100 picnic tables spread across oaks, pastures and a goat pen. Order a wood-fired pizza and a farmhouse ale (or a soda for the kids), then claim a table facing the open land as the light turns gold. Families and leashed dogs are welcome all day, and the grounds are big enough for children to roam. It is walk-in only with no reservations, so arrive early on weekend afternoons.

Mon to Thu 2 to 8 pm, Fri 2 to 9 pm, Sat 11 am to 9 pm, Sun 11 am to 8 pm (jesterkingbrewery.com).

Dogs welcome across the grounds; the brewery requires them to stay on a leash and asks you to pick up after them.

 Pro Tip

No reservations, so claim a table by 4 pm on weekends and bring a layer; the open ridge cools quickly after sunset. The kitchen and taproom close by 8 or 9 pm.

No. 02

The Broken Spoke

$$ 10 min drive Two-step lesson and dinner

Austin's last true Texas dance hall, open since 1964 on South Lamar, with a neon sign, vintage cars out front and a restaurant known for chicken fried steak. Come early for free live music in the restaurant from 6 to 8 pm, then stay for the two-step and swing lesson at 8 pm before the dance hall bands start at 9. It is a place to watch couples spin and let kids soak up real Texas tradition. Eat dinner here before the late dance sets.

Tue to Thu 4 pm to midnight, Fri and Sat 4 pm to 1:30 am; kitchen closes 10 pm (Tue to Thu) or 11 pm (Fri, Sat); dance lessons Wed to Sat 8 to 9 pm (brokenspokeaustintx.net).

 Pro Tip

The family-friendly window is dinner plus the free 6 to 8 pm music and the 8 pm dance lesson; the dance hall bands start at 9 pm and run until 1 am. The venue's own site states no age policy, so call 512-442-6189 before bringing children.

No. 03

Mount Bonnell (Covert Park)

Free 20 min drive Free golden-hour view

The classic Austin sunset overlook: about 100 stone steps lead to a limestone bluff above the Colorado River, with views across Lake Austin and out toward the Pennybacker Bridge. Locals bring a blanket and a thermos or a bottle of something cold and watch the hills turn pink. The climb is short but steep, so wear real shoes, and keep young children close to the unfenced edges. Parking is free but limited, so arrive well before golden hour.

Park hours 5 am to 10 pm daily per city sources; no parking overnight.

 Pro Tip

Arrive 45 minutes before sunset to get a parking spot, and keep valuables out of sight, since car break-ins have been reported in the lot. The park closes at 10 pm.

No. 04

Auditorium Shores at Lady Bird Lake

Free 15 min drive Skyline sunset picnic

A wide lawn on the south shore of Lady Bird Lake with the downtown skyline across the water, which turns coppery at sunset. Spread a blanket on the grass, watch paddleboarders and rowers glide past, and walk the Ann and Roy Butler Hike and Bike Trail. It is free, flat and stroller friendly, and there is an off-leash dog area at the same park. Bring your own drinks and snacks; there is no restaurant on site.

City parks are open 5 am to 10 pm daily unless otherwise posted.

Off-leash area at the park (about 920 W Riverside Dr); the City says dogs must stay within sight and sound control of their handler, and the area is not fenced next to a busy road.

 Pro Tip

Park at the Palmer Events Center side or walk across the Congress Avenue or First Street bridges from downtown; the lawn fills quickly on festival weekends.

No. 05

Cidercade Austin

$$ 10 min drive Arcade night for families

A big arcade bar on East Riverside with free-play games, craft cider and artisan pizza, just across the road from Lady Bird Lake. Before 9 pm everyone is welcome, so families can play pinball and classic cabinets while adults sip cider and kids order unlimited soda. Guests under 16 must stay with a parent or guardian at all times. After 9 pm it turns strictly adults only, so plan to finish up in the early evening.

10 am to midnight daily, strictly adults only after 9 pm; closed Thanksgiving and Christmas Day, open 10 am to 6 pm Christmas Eve (cidercade.com/austin/faq).

Dogs are not allowed inside; the venue says it is friendly to dogs but does not let them in.

 Pro Tip

Pay once at the door and every game is free; go after a lakeside sunset walk and be out by 9 pm, when it becomes adults only. Kids ages 5 and up pay $12 admission.

Questions about Austin sunsets and live music

What time is sunset in Austin in winter?

Around 5:30 pm. Go to Jester King, Mount Bonnell or Auditorium Shores in the middle to late afternoon so you are settled before the light changes.

Are these places good for kids?

Jester King welcomes families and leashed dogs. Cidercade lets under-16s in with a parent and is strictly adults only after 9 pm. The Broken Spoke does not state an age policy, so call first and go for dinner and the early dance lesson.

Where can I take my dog at sunset?

Auditorium Shores has an off-leash area, and Jester King welcomes leashed dogs across its grounds. Dogs are not allowed inside Cidercade.

Can I still see the Austin bats in winter?

No. The bats at the South Congress Bridge are a seasonal sight that runs roughly March to November, so it is a spring to fall evening plan.
