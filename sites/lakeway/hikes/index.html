Top 5 Hikes
Near Lakeway

Neighborhood walks to bigger creek hikes, with dog rules spelled out.

The best hikes near Lakeway are Lakeway City Park for easy walks, Hamilton Greenbelt for a shaded creek walk, Bee Cave Central Park for a paved loop and dog park, Commons Ford Ranch Metropolitan Park for quiet trails, and the Barton Creek Greenbelt 360 trailhead for a bigger rocky hike. Drive times run under 5 to 35 minutes from the house.

Most of these are a short drive from the house, and the first two are close enough to walk to. We say where dogs must stay leashed and where there is an off-leash area. Closures are current as of the date below, so check the park page before you drive. We take no commissions. Drive times are from the house.

No. 01

Lakeway City Park

Free 10 min walk easy walks with dogs and kids

The easiest walk on the list and the closest one to the house. A 64-acre lakeside park with nearly two miles of connected paved pathway plus a walking trail and a nature trail, a beach, a butterfly garden, a wildflower meadow and the lake-view Huser Memorial Pavilion. It works for a quick dog lap before breakfast or a stroller loop after dinner. Playgrounds, restrooms and a fenced dog park are on site, and there is no admission charge.

Daily 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. during Daylight Saving Time, 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. during Standard Time (the city's own page).

Dogs are welcome and must be under physical control (leashed) on trails and grounds; the fenced Bark Park inside City Park is the designated off-leash area (city page lists the dog park; leash rule per Lakeway's leash law).

 Pro Tip

Leash up for the trails and use the fenced Bark Park for off-leash play; the city lets dogs run loose only in designated areas. The route from the house is about half a mile, so walking works.

No. 02

Hamilton Greenbelt

Free under 5 min drive shaded creek walk to waterfall

Lakeway's most-used trail, following Hurst Creek and Yaupon Creek through shaded cedar elm and live oak. Walkers reach a small circular waterfall in roughly 15 minutes, with ferns, mossy rocks and boulder fields along the way. From the Palos Verdes Drive entrance, primitive trails drop down toward the Hurst Creek arm of Lake Travis, a rockier and steeper stretch. Maps are posted at both entrances, and parking is free at each.

Dogs are welcome and must be on a leash (city's Hamilton Greenbelts page).

 Pro Tip

Start at the Lohmans Crossing entrance for the easy waterfall walk. Save the Palos Verdes entrance and its lake-bound primitive trails for sturdy shoes and a longer morning.

No. 03

Bee Cave Central Park

Free 15 min drive flat paved loop with dog park

A 50-acre park with a paved perimeter trail of just over a mile, flat and stroller-friendly, so it suits anyone who wants a gentle stretch without hills. It has shaded play structures, restrooms and water fountains. Dogs get their own fenced dog park below the small pavilion, with separate areas for small and large dogs, drinking water and shaded seating. The Galleria shops and coffee are a short drive away for a post-walk stop.

A fenced off-leash dog park with separate small and large dog areas sits below the small pavilion (city page); dogs must be non-aggressive. Leash rule for the perimeter trail is not stated by the city.

 Pro Tip

Use the dog park for off-leash time, and keep dogs on a leash on the perimeter trail, since the city's page does not mention an off-leash trail allowance. Phone is the city main line.

No. 04

Commons Ford Ranch Metropolitan Park

Free 30 min drive quiet trails, picnic, ranch ruins

A Lake Austin park where the trails cross prairie, canyon and chaparral before reaching the water. Historic buildings from the former Resaca Ranch, including a barn and ranch house, still stand on site, and there are picnic areas, a boat dock and a swimming area. Bring a picnic and plan an unhurried half day. Leash required throughout, and glass and smoking are banned.

The park page lists no hours; Austin parks are generally open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. unless posted (city off-leash page).

Pets must stay on a leash and under the owner's control at all times (City of Austin park page).

 Pro Tip

Swimming and the boat ramp are closed through November 30, 2026 because Lake Austin is being lowered, so go for the trails. The city page does not list an entrance fee.

No. 05

Barton Creek Greenbelt (360 Trailhead)

Free 35 min drive bigger rocky creek hike

Austin's best-known hike: 12-plus miles of rocky creekside trail, with a main trail of 7.5 miles. The 360 Trailhead has a free lot and composting restrooms, making it the easiest way in from the Lake Travis side. Terrain is rough and uneven, so wear real shoes, and carry water. There is almost no lighting, so finish before sunset.

Trail hours are not listed; all park facilities close after 10 p.m. per city ordinance.

Dogs are allowed on a leash of 6 feet or shorter; the city page lists no off-leash areas.

 Pro Tip

Creek levels can rise quickly even on dry-looking days, so check USGS or LCRA gauges for Barton Creek before heading out.

Questions about hikes near Lakeway

Where can I let my dog run off-leash near Lakeway?

Lakeway City Park has a fenced Bark Park, and Bee Cave Central Park has a fenced off-leash dog park with separate small and large dog areas. Dogs must be leashed on the trails at Hamilton Greenbelt and Commons Ford.

Are any of these parks closed right now?

At Commons Ford Ranch, the boat ramp is closed and swimming is prohibited through Nov 30, 2026, because of the Lake Austin drawdown. The Barton Creek Greenbelt can rise quickly after rain and limit access.

Which hike is closest to the house?

Lakeway City Park and Hamilton Greenbelt are both under 5 minutes by car, and City Park is about a 10-minute walk. The city lists City Park hours as 6 am to 8 pm during Standard Time.

Do these hikes cost anything?

All five are listed as free. The Commons Ford city page lists no fee but also no hours, so check it before you go.
