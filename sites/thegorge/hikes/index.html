No. 01

Cave B Waterfall Loop Trail (Sagecliffe)

Free 5 min walk short waterfall hike near the house

The closest hike to the house, a marked, sandy trail from the Sagecliffe resort down off the plateau into the gorge, past basalt caves and Fitzpatrick Waterfall, which the resort says runs year-round. The trail follows the bluff edge with close views of the cliffs and the Columbia River, and climbs back out past Cattail Meadow with the Gorge Amphitheatre above. The resort lists 2.5 to 3 hours for the loop; the host measures it at about 2.8 miles out and back, and published reports say about 2.5. The descent is the steepest part, and afternoons are windy.

Trail guide gives 2.5 to 3 hours for the Waterfall Loop; it is a marked trail and the resort's guide does not state seasonal closures. The resort's guide is an older document, so confirm current trail status with the resort.

 Pro Tip

The resort's own trail guide warns that poison oak grows thickly around the waterfall and stream, so keep to the path. It also advises sturdy closed-toe shoes, lots of water and hiking early in the morning in summer. The trail is on resort property: ask the resort about access and parking before you go. Ticks and rattlesnakes are on all area trails, so check legs, clothes and dogs afterward.

No. 02

Ancient Lakes and Dusty Lake (Quincy Lakes Unit)

$ 25 min drive lakes, cliffs and spring waterfalls

A walk down the Babcock Bench through basalt coulees to a string of small lakes, with cliffs on both sides. Most people take the main trail to Ancient Lakes, and the more adventurous add Dusty Lake by a steeper, unsigned route over talus; the host's loop is about 5.4 miles, while published reports of the Dusty Lake and Ancient Lakes combinations run from about 5 to 7 miles, so plan for the longer end. Lake water is untested, so carry your own, and there is little shade. Winter is cool and quiet; summer is hot, so start at sunrise.

Foot traffic is allowed year-round. WDFW closes the main access road to vehicles from October 1 to the end of February (its pages differ between Feb 28 and Mar 1), so winter hikes can start at the gate and add distance each way. Confirm the current gate status on the WDFW page before you drive.

Dogs are allowed on a leash per Washington Trails Association (WDFW's page lists no dog rule); keep them close because of rattlesnakes.

 Pro Tip

A Discover Pass is required to park (WDFW; buy at the pay station on site, $10 a day or $30 to $45 for a year). Ticks and rattlesnakes are present on all area trails: stay on the trail, wear closed shoes and check clothes and dogs afterward. Download the map before you go, since the route has no signage and cell service is poor.

No. 03

Burke Lake and the Quincy Lakes Road (WDFW Quincy Lakes Unit)

$ 15 min drive easy winter walk to a lake

A quiet walk along the gravel Quincy Lakes Road to Burke Lake, one of the string of small lakes the Ice Age floods left in the channeled scablands west of Quincy. WDFW's Burke Lake West access sits 1.5 miles in from the gate, with basalt rims, marsh edges and plenty of waterbirds in the cool months. Because the road is closed to cars from October 1 to the end of February, winter visitors walk in, which makes it a flat, easy 3-mile round trip and a good option when other trails are icy. There is a vault toilet at the lake, and the rest of the unit's trail network branches off from here.

Open year-round for people on foot. WDFW states the access is closed to vehicles October 1 to February 28 (its main unit page says the hunting section ends March 1), so from now into late winter you walk the 1.5 miles from the gate. Walking in is allowed; confirm the gate location on the WDFW page.

Dogs are allowed on a leash (Washington Trails Association's Quincy Lakes entry; WDFW's own pages list no dog rule). Keep them close because of rattlesnakes.

 Pro Tip

Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife requires a Discover Pass on its lands (a hunting or fishing Vehicle Access Pass also works), and there is a pay station on the unit; if you walk in from the gate in winter, park at the gate and check the WDFW page for signs. Ticks and rattlesnakes live on the trails in this area (the host warns about both), so stay on the road or path, wear closed shoes and check clothes and dogs afterward. Fall is bird-hunting season on this unit, so wear bright colors and check WDFW season dates.

No. 04

Ginkgo Petrified Forest State Park

$ 30 min drive petrified wood, river views, easy walk

A state park on the Columbia River at Vantage where fossilized logs from an ancient forest lie where they fell. The Trees of Stone Interpretive Trail is 1.25 miles through petrified logs in their original setting, and the park has about three miles of trails in all. Indoor displays cover the petrified forest and the Ice Age floods, and you can touch petrified wood outside. Allow an hour or two, and bring a windbreaker, since the river wind off the cliffs is strong.

Park hours in winter are 8 a.m. to dusk (6:30 a.m. to dusk in summer). The Interpretive Center is open 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Friday to Sunday through Dec 23, closed Nov 27, then Sundays only from Dec 24, 2026 to Mar 31, 2027; free admission (parks.wa.gov, seen 2026-11-25).

Dogs are allowed on a leash (Washington State Parks).

 Pro Tip

A Discover Pass ($10 a day or $45 a year, per Washington State Parks) is needed for the vehicle. Exhibits are inside the interpretive center, which is open only a few days a week in winter, so check its schedule and plan the walk for the middle of the day. Watch for rattlesnakes and ticks off the paved path.

No. 05

Wild Horses Monument (Grandfather Cuts Loose the Ponies)

Free 35 min drive quick stop with big views

Fifteen life-size steel horses appear to gallop along a ridge above the Columbia River, one of the best-known sights near Vantage. A short, moderately steep trail of sand and loose rock climbs from the parking lot to the sculptures, about 0.4 miles round trip with 150 feet of gain. Trekking poles help on the loose surface. The lot holds about 25 cars and has no restrooms, so pair it with Ginkgo Petrified Forest State Park, which is about ten minutes away.

 Pro Tip

Exit 139 is reached only from eastbound I-90. Westbound drivers must exit at Vantage (136) or Gorge (143) and turn around. Late-afternoon light on the horses is the best for photos. Stay on the main trail to limit erosion, and watch for rattlesnakes and ticks.

Questions about hikes near The Gorge

Do I need a pass for these trails?

A Discover Pass is required for Ancient Lakes, the Quincy Lakes road and Ginkgo Petrified Forest State Park. Wild Horses Monument needs no pass, per the Washington Trails Association.

Are the trails open in winter?

Yes, on foot. The state closes the Ancient Lakes and Quincy Lakes access roads to vehicles from October 1 to late February, so winter hikes start at the gate and add distance each way. Ginkgo's interpretive center is open Sundays only from Dec 24 to March 31.

Can I bring my dog on these hikes?

Dogs are allowed on a leash at Ancient Lakes, Burke Lake and Ginkgo Petrified Forest State Park. Keep them close because of rattlesnakes and ticks. The Cave B and Wild Horses cards do not state a dog rule.

Is the Cave B waterfall trail marked?

It is a marked loop that the resort's trail guide estimates at 2.5 to 3 hours, and it warns about poison oak. Check with the resort for current trail status, since the guide is older.
