Top 5 Hikes
Near Chelan

A viewpoint climb, a river gorge, an easy loop and a winter snowshoe ridge.

Our top five hikes in Chelan, WA are the Chelan Butte Trail to Elephant Head, the Chelan Gorge Trail, Chelan Riverwalk Park, Little Bear Trail at Lake Chelan State Park and Echo Ridge Recreation Area. They range from a paved one-mile loop to a steep viewpoint climb and a winter snowshoe day.

We recommend these five because most are free, close to town and doable in a few hours. Year round, the Butte, the Gorge, Riverwalk and Little Bear stay open, while Echo Ridge is a summer hike that becomes a snowshoe area only once snow holds, so check before you drive up.

No. 01

Chelan Butte Trail to Elephant Head

Free 4 min drive big views, close to town

This is the locals' view hike: a steady, dusty climb up a jeep track from the edge of town to Elephant Head, a rocky viewpoint with panoramic views and the first look at the Columbia River. The Elephant Head spur is short but very steep, with a couple of benches along the way. The full trail continues to the 2,500-foot-gain summit, so most families turn around at the spur. There is no shade at all, and dogs on leash are welcome.

 Pro Tip

Cross the road from the lot and take the unmarked jeep track, not the paved walking paths by the parking area. In summer start at sunrise and carry 2 liters of water per person, since hikers have turned back from the heat. Rattlesnakes have been reported on the trail in warm months. In winter, expect snow or ice on the upper trail and stop at the Elephant Head spur if it looks slick.

No. 02

Chelan Gorge Trail (Reach 1)

Free 5 min drive easy river walk, all ages

A Chelan PUD trail system that drops from a wide, packed-gravel path into the short Chelan River gorge, where eagles, deer and the occasional bighorn sheep show up on the cliffs. The first 0.4 mile is nearly flat with interpretive signs, so strollers and little legs can do an easy out-and-back. Past that, the single-track Eagle and Coyote trails descend to a cobbly river plain with gorge views. There is no shade, and leashed dogs are welcome.

 Pro Tip

Walk only the flat first 0.4 mile with young kids, because the descent to the river is steep and the river cobbles are slippery. In winter, trip reports describe the gravel as in good shape with little mud, but after heavy snow locals walk the county road instead of the trails. The shared lot holds only about a dozen cars and also serves the Chelan Butte trail, so go early on sunny weekends.

No. 03

Chelan Riverwalk Park and Reach 1 Loop

Free About 20 min walk easy stroller-friendly stroll

The gentlest option on the list: a one-mile paved loop along the Chelan River through a 12-acre Chelan PUD park with shaded benches, picnic tables, a playfield and restrooms. It sits right in town, so it works as a short stroll before dinner or a stroller-friendly morning walk. Fit walkers can link to the 3-mile round-trip Reach 1 trail from the boat launch for gorge-edge views, mostly flat at the start.

 Pro Tip

Restrooms are in the park, and the trailhead lot for Chelan Butte has none, so stop here first. Combine the one-mile loop with the Reach 1 trail only in dry, ice-free weather, because the descent to the river is steep.

No. 04

Little Bear Trail at Lake Chelan State Park

$ 19 min drive easy loop with lake views

A 2.3-mile loop with about 250 feet of gain through ponderosa pine savanna above the lake, with benches at viewpoints and interpretive signs along the way. WTA rates it easy and good for kids. The state park around it has a sandy beach, playground and restrooms, so you can add a lakeside picnic after the walk. Dogs are welcome on leash, and in spring the meadows fill with wildflowers.

Hours and season: 6:30 a.m. to dusk, year round (parks.wa.gov).

 Pro Tip

Bring a Discover Pass ($10 day, $45 annual at the state parks page) for the parking lot, and buy it online before you leave since cell service is unreliable on the road. Park hours run 6:30am to dusk year round, so plan a late-morning winter walk to have full daylight.

No. 05

Echo Ridge Recreation Area (summer hikes, winter snowshoe)

$ About 28 min drive snowshoe day or summer ridge hike

A ridge-top trail network about 30 minutes above Chelan that is the one hike here that suits both guest seasons. In summer the open pine and sagebrush trails are easy family hiking with wide views over the Columbia Basin. In winter it becomes a Nordic ski area with a separate set of marked snowshoe trails, about 9 miles per one source. Snowshoers and dogs stay off the groomed ski tracks.

Hours and season: Open 24/7 while snow holds, typically to mid-March. As of November 23 the Lake Chelan Nordic Ski Club lists the winter area as not yet open, waiting for snow. Summer pass rules: confirm with the Chelan Ranger District, 509-682-4900.

 Pro Tip

In winter, buy your day pass online from the Lake Chelan Nordic Ski Club before you go ($15 per adult, ages 17 and under free per KPQ), since Northwest Forest and Sno-Park passes are not accepted there. Start from the Shoe trailhead for snowshoe routes and carry chains, because the road can be very icy.

Questions about hiking near Chelan

Are the Chelan hikes open in winter?

Chelan Butte, the Gorge Trail, Riverwalk Park and Little Bear Trail are open through winter, though the Butte can be icy or snowy on the upper trail. Echo Ridge turns into a snowshoe and Nordic area once snow arrives, typically mid-December to March.

Which Chelan hike is best with young kids?

We recommend the Chelan Riverwalk Park loop, a paved one-mile loop with restrooms and picnic tables. The first 0.4 mile of the Chelan Gorge Trail is also flat and stroller friendly. Little Bear Trail is a rated-easy 2.3-mile loop.

Do I need a pass for these hikes?

Chelan Butte, the Gorge Trail and Riverwalk Park are free. Little Bear Trail needs a Discover Pass for parking ($10 day, $45 annual). In winter, Echo Ridge needs a Lake Chelan Nordic Ski Club day pass ($15 per adult, ages 17 and under free).

Can I snowshoe near Chelan?

Yes, at Echo Ridge Recreation Area, about 28 minutes from east downtown Chelan, on marked snowshoe trails kept apart from the groomed ski tracks. It opens only when snow holds and was not yet open on November 23, 2026. Check lakechelannordic.org first.

Why isn't Beebe Springs on this list?

Beebe Springs Wildlife Area is closed to public access from December 1 to March 15, which covers most winter stays, and it also requires a Discover Pass. We left it off so every pick here is open when you visit.
