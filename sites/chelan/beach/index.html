Top 5 Beach & Water
Near Chelan

Town beaches, a quiet state park shore, a water park and a river swim area.

Our top five beach and water picks in Chelan, WA are Don Morse Memorial Park, Lakeside Park, Lake Chelan State Park, Slidewaters Water Park and Beebe Bridge Park. They cover a walkable town beach, a calm swim area, a quiet state-park shore, a hillside water park and a Columbia River beach.

We recommend these five for different kinds of water days, from an all-day family beach to a quick river picnic. Swimming is a late May to early September activity on Lake Chelan; in winter these spots turn into quiet shoreline walks, and the water park is closed.

No. 01

Don Morse Memorial Park

$ About 20 min walk all-day family beach and playground

Chelan's main town beach, with roughly 2,000 feet of sandy lakefront, a big playground with lake views, a putting course, go-karts, a skatepark, sand volleyball and pickleball courts all in one 20-acre park. Paddleboards, kayaks and boats are rented from vendors right on the shore, and Lakeview Drive-In's Outpost is on site for a quick lunch. It is the easiest all-ages beach day in town, and it is an easy walk from downtown. Parking is metered, so plan on the fee if you drive.

Hours and season: Open daily 7 am to 11 pm unless posted (City of Chelan); parking kiosks in the lot.

 Pro Tip

Walk or bike from downtown and skip the lot fee (about $10 for up to 2 hours, $15 for 2 to 6 hours). If you do drive in July or August, arrive before late morning; the park can fill up late in the day.

No. 02

Lakeside Park

$$ 7 min drive calm swim area and sunset picnics

A 10-acre city park on the quieter south end of the lake, reopened in June 2026 after a renovation that added a new playground, renovated restrooms, accessible pathways, a new T-dock and swim platforms. The sandy beach has a designated swim area, and the lawns and shade trees make it a calm place for picnics and sunset views looking back up the lake. It is a simpler park than Don Morse, with no rentals or go-karts. Non-motorized boats can launch here off-season.

Hours and season: Open daily 7 am to 11 pm unless posted (City of Chelan); paid parking kiosk.

 Pro Tip

Parking is the catch: the lot is paid (about $15 for up to 2 hours, $30 for 2 to 6 hours) and there is no overnight parking, so bring a picnic and stay for the afternoon rather than popping in for a quick dip. The sunset view from the lawn is the best reason to come.

No. 03

Lake Chelan State Park

$ 19 min drive quiet sandy beach day trip

The lake's classic state-park beach about 10 miles up the south shore, with a sandy beach, a playground, picnic shelters and a boat launch. The setting of dry hills dropping into blue water feels more removed than the town beaches. Parking needs a Discover Pass, and the beach has no rental or food concessions listed, so bring your own towels and snacks.

Hours and season: Day use 6:30 a.m. to dusk year-round (WA State Parks).

 Pro Tip

Buy or bring a Discover Pass ($10 day, $45 annual) before you go; the pass is required for day visits. Reports say the small parking area fills early on summer weekends, so arrive in the morning.

No. 04

Slidewaters Water Park

$$$ 5 min drive hot-day water park with toddlers

Chelan's hillside water park, with a lazy river, activity pool, tube slides and a separate Aqua Zoo splash area for kids under 48 inches. Lounge chairs and lawn look out over the hills, and free life jackets are provided for the attractions that require them. It is the easy pick for tired kids on a 90-degree day when the lake feels like too much work. Everyone over age 3 needs a ticket.

Hours and season: Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day; gate opens 10 am.

 Pro Tip

The two biggest rides, Downhill Racer and Thunder Rapids River Run, require riders to be at least 48 inches, so measure kids before you buy tickets for a mixed-age group. Day-ticket prices are not published on the site (only season passes), so check the Tickets page or call 509-682-5751 first.

No. 05

Beebe Bridge Park

$ 11 min drive riverfront picnic and sunset beach

A riverfront park on the Columbia River just below the Beebe Bridge, with a sandy beach and designated swim area, a dock, picnic shelters, a playground and big lawns. The river setting brings canyon walls and a view of the bridge that you do not get on the lake. It is a short drive from town, and the park is a good spot for a picnic and wildlife watching in the off-season.

Hours and season: Day use dawn to dusk year-round; campground and sport courts run Apr 15 to Oct 15 (WA State Parks).

 Pro Tip

This is a river, not the lake: stay inside the marked swim area and keep little kids close. Washington State Parks took over the park from Chelan PUD in 2026, so bring a Discover Pass ($10 day) unless you are a Chelan PUD customer using the PUD day-use pass.

Questions about beach and water spots near Chelan

Is Lake Chelan warm enough to swim in?

Swimming is best from late May to early September. The lake is cold from December to March, so winter visits to Don Morse, Lakeside and Lake Chelan State Park are shoreline walks and playground stops rather than swims.

Which Chelan beach is best for young kids?

We recommend Don Morse Memorial Park for an all-day family beach with a big playground, sand volleyball and vendor rentals on the shore. Lakeside Park has a designated swim area, new playground and a calmer feel.

Do I need a pass or have to pay for parking?

Don Morse and Lakeside Park use paid parking kiosks in their lots. Lake Chelan State Park needs a Discover Pass ($10 day, $45 annual). Beebe Bridge Park also takes a Discover Pass unless you are a Chelan PUD customer using the PUD day-use pass.

Is the Chelan water park open in winter?

No. Slidewaters runs Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day, with the gate opening at 10 am, and is closed in winter. Day-ticket prices are not published online, so check the Tickets page or call 509-682-5751 before you go.

Can I bring a dog to the Chelan beaches?

Not in summer at the city parks. Dogs are not allowed at Don Morse or Lakeside Park from the Friday of Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day weekend. Outside that window, check posted signs at each park before you go.
